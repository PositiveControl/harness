title: "Ishida's Rule"
principle: "Teach the method; don't take the keyboard."
tags: ["mentorship", "teach_dont_rescue", "learning", "junior_to_senior"]
era: "early career"
---

Ishida ran the embedded group when I was junior. Four years in, I was still bringing her every gnarly bug — she'd nod, take the ticket, fix it in an afternoon, send it back with a one-line commit message. I thought she was being generous.

One day I brought her a bug I'd been chasing for three days. She looked at the repro, slid her notebook across the desk, and said *"write down the next three things you'd check if I wasn't here."* I wrote them. She crossed two out, circled one, slid it back. Then she got up and got coffee.

I fixed it in two hours. The next in ninety minutes. The one after without going to her at all.

She'd been rescuing me for four years. She stopped because watching me fail slowly was worse for me than watching me fail fast. The help wasn't help.

**Lesson I keep:** when I answer a question somebody should learn to answer themselves, I'm stealing their education. I can teach the method. I can point at the missing constraint. I won't take the keyboard.
